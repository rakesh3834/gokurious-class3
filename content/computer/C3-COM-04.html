What’s going on here?

A keyboard gives you a way to enter letters and numbers. Each letter has a key, and the digits zero to nine have keys too. Pressing keys in a particular order makes a word or a number. SUN needs S, then U, then N. Reversing the order makes a different string of letters.

In the keyboard garden, a target appears above your work. You can use the labelled keys on the page or type in the practice box. Look for one character at a time. You do not have to hurry. This activity has no countdown, and a short, careful attempt is more useful than rushing through several wrong keys.

Every character you enter appears in your key line. A matching character in the matching position gets a sprout. A leaf marks a character that differs from the target at that position. The characters themselves remain visible, so you can compare the target and your work without relying only on the picture or its colour.

Use Backspace to remove the last character if you need another try. Clear typing removes the whole attempt. Change the target to explore a familiar word, a short number or a mixture such as SUN3. Notice that the final 3 is a digit, while S, U and N are letters. Both kinds of keys can appear in the same message.

The practice box shows letters in capitals to keep the matching task simple. It accepts letters and digits for this garden, rather than every symbol a real keyboard can enter. Later activities explore other keys and letter case. For now, say the target, find its characters and enter them in order. When every character matches, explain which keys were letters and which were digits.

Learning outcomeI can locate and use letters and digits on a keyboard.

Little questions, big discoveries

Must I type quickly?

No. There is no timer. Look carefully and use as much time as you need.

Why are my typed letters shown as capitals?

This garden uses capitals for simple matching. Another lesson explores Caps Lock and Shift.

Can I use a touch screen?

Yes. Tap the letter and digit buttons on the page instead of using a physical keyboard.

Talk about it: parents & teachers

At home

  • Invite your child to choose a short familiar word and say each letter before entering it.
  • Let your child explain one change in the model before offering help. Give time for another attempt.

In the classroom

  • Provide target cards with familiar words and numbers up to three digits. Let partners check the order together.
  • Ask pairs to predict, take turns with the controls, and describe the evidence they observed. Use the three challenges to identify a point to revisit.
