Colour Match Workshop

Make kite regions match by sampling a colour and filling a different closed region.

Wonder about it: Can you give two kite sections exactly the same colour?

What’s going on here?

A drawing can contain separate areas surrounded by boundaries. We call these closed regions when the boundary goes all the way around. A fill tool can colour one such region. In our kite, four closed triangular regions meet in the middle. Each region has a number and a colour name so you can identify it in more than one way.

Start with the Fill tool. Choose a colour swatch and tap a region of the kite, or choose its numbered button. That region changes to the selected colour. The other regions stay as they were. The boundary tells this model which area to fill. Filling a region is different from drawing a narrow stroke along the path of a brush.

Suppose you want another region to match a colour already in the picture. Choose Pick colour, then tap the region with the colour you want. The selected colour label changes to the sampled colour. Picking does not repaint that region. It prepares the colour for another action, just as choosing a crayon gets it ready for use.

Switch back to Fill and choose a different region. The second region now uses the same colour you sampled. Compare the two names and look at the picture. If you pick from the yellow region, then fill the blue region, both can become yellow. Try changing a region first and sampling its new colour to see that the picker reads the current picture.

Our kite uses clear, closed regions so the result is easy to follow. In other drawing programs, a gap in a boundary can let a fill reach an area you did not intend. Here the boundaries stay closed throughout the activity. Practise the sequence aloud: pick a colour from the picture, choose Fill, then choose the destination region. Reset brings back four different starting colours.

Make it real

Matching kite colours

You need: Paper and four crayons

  1. Draw a kite divided into four closed regions.
  2. Colour two regions differently.
  3. Choose one existing colour and use it in a third region.

Notice which two regions now match exactly.

Little questions, big discoveries

Does picking change the picture?

No. It changes the selected colour. Fill applies that colour to a region.

Can I use the numbered buttons?

Yes. They select the same kite regions as tapping the picture and include colour names.

What happens when I pick a repainted region?

The picker samples its current colour, including a colour you have just filled there.

Talk about it: parents & teachers

At home

  • Ask your child to describe the three steps for making two regions match before testing them.
  • Let your child explain one change in the model before offering help. Give time for another attempt.

In the classroom

  • Have pairs alternate between sampler and filler roles, using region numbers to make their instructions precise.
  • Ask pairs to predict, take turns with the controls, and describe the evidence they observed. Use the three challenges to identify a point to revisit.
