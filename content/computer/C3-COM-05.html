What’s going on here?

When you edit a message, the cursor shows where the next change will happen. It is the upright marker between characters or at an end of the text. A letter appears at that place. Backspace removes the character just before it. Moving the cursor lets you repair one part without starting the whole message again.

Our starting message is catssleepz. The goal is Cats on the first line and sleep on the next line. Begin at the end and remove the extra z with Backspace. Then use the arrow controls to move between the words. Enter starts a new line at the cursor. Space adds a gap on the same line, so these two keys have different jobs.

To repair the first letter, move to the start, move just after c and use Backspace. The cursor is now ready for a new first letter. Shift prepares one capital letter in this model. Choose Shift and then C to insert a capital C. Shift switches off after that letter, so the following letters can be small again.

Caps Lock is another way to control letter case. It stays on until you switch it off. With Caps Lock on, letters are capitals; using Shift at the same time reverses the case for one letter in this model. Try these controls separately first. Watch the case labels so you know which mode is ready before pressing a letter.

Choose a prediction before an edit: will the key move the cursor, add something, remove something or change a case mode? Compare your prediction with the visible result. Home and End help you jump to the beginning or end. At the very beginning, Backspace has no earlier character to remove. Careful editing is about position and purpose, not speed.

Learning outcomeI can use special keys to correct a short message.

  3. Replace c with C and split the words into two lines.

Notice that the same key action depends on the cursor position.

Little questions, big discoveries

Can Backspace remove a letter after the cursor?

Backspace removes the character before the cursor. Move the cursor just after the character you want to remove.

Does Shift stay on forever here?

No. It is ready for one letter, then turns off. Caps Lock stays on until toggled off.

Why did an arrow not change my letters?

Arrow keys move the cursor. They let you choose a position without changing the letters themselves.

Talk about it: parents & teachers

At home

  • Ask your child to explain what the cursor is beside before pressing an editing key.
  • Let your child explain one change in the model before offering help. Give time for another attempt.

In the classroom

  • Model one repair with paper letters, then let pupils predict each edit before using the on-screen keys.
  • Ask pairs to predict, take turns with the controls, and describe the evidence they observed. Use the three challenges to identify a point to revisit.
