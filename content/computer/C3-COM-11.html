Move the Flower

What’s going on here?

A picture contains many tiny coloured points called pixels. A rectangular selection chooses all the pixels inside its boundary. That includes the object you want and any background around it. Selecting is like placing a paper window over a picture and saying, “This whole area is the part I want to work with.”

Our flower begins on a white background. The target is an indigo dashed box on a pale green area. Choose Draw selection and drag from one corner to another around the flower. The amber dashed boundary shows the selected rectangle. Check that you included the petals and the bottom of the stem before deciding that the selection is ready.

Choose Move selected region, then drag to move it. You can also use the direction buttons. The pixels inside the rectangle move together, and their old area becomes white. If your selection included white background, that white background moves too. You may see a white rectangle cover part of the pale green target. This is evidence of what your boundary included.

Reset before trying another boundary. The Tight flower selection button chooses the flower with a small amount of surrounding white space. The Wide flower selection button chooses more background. These buttons let you compare the same idea without needing to drag precisely. Move each selection and describe how much extra white area travels with the flower.

A useful selection includes all of the object you need, with as little unwanted background as possible. Too small a selection can leave petals or a stem behind. Too wide a selection brings extra background and may not fit the target. Moving is different from zooming: moving changes where selected pixels are in the picture. Try a tighter boundary and explain the improvement using what you can see.

Make it real

Move a paper picture region

You need: Paper, crayons and two rectangular paper frames

  1. Draw a flower on a lightly shaded background.
  2. Place a large frame around it and point to all the background inside.
  3. Use a smaller frame that still includes the whole flower.

Notice how the chosen region changes even though the flower is the same.

Little questions, big discoveries

Why did a white rectangle cover the target?

White background inside the selection moved with the flower. It is part of the selected region.

Can I practise without drawing a precise boundary?

Yes. Choose Tight flower selection or Wide flower selection, then use the direction buttons.

Why will my selection not move farther?

It has reached the picture edge. The model keeps the whole selected rectangle inside the canvas.

Talk about it: parents & teachers

At home

  • Use a paper frame to discuss what is inside and outside a selected area before moving the model flower.
  • Let your child explain one change in the model before offering help. Give time for another attempt.

In the classroom

  • Compare wide and tight selections after a reset. Ask learners to point to evidence that background moved.
  • Ask pairs to predict, take turns with the controls, and describe the evidence they observed. Use the three challenges to identify a point to revisit.
