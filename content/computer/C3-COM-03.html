Ready, Work, Rest

Guide a pretend computer through a supervised start, a small drawing and a careful shutdown.

What’s going on here?

Before using a computer at school, check that an adult says it is ready. Your teacher may have a routine for the devices in your room. Follow that routine. Adults handle plugs, power supplies and loose cables. A child can practise the order of a session without touching any of those things.

Our computer is a model on a page. Begin by arranging three start cards: ask an adult to check readiness, press the pretend power button and wait for the desktop. Waiting matters because a computer needs time to get ready. Repeatedly pressing buttons does not help it finish starting. Watch the step label to see what happens next.

After the start plan is ready, use the controls to carry it out. Confirm that the adult has checked readiness, press pretend power and choose the wait step. When the desktop is ready, make the supplied drawing. The drawing is your work for this short practice session. Now think about what should happen before you finish.

Saving keeps the practice drawing ready before you close it. Close the saved drawing, choose the practice Shut down menu action and wait until the model says it is off safely. Saving, closing a drawing and shutting down a computer are different actions. The visible sequence helps you notice the purpose of each one instead of treating them as one button.

Try an action too early and read the explanation. The model asks you to complete the step that belongs first. On a real computer, the menu names and exact start or shutdown steps can differ, so follow your adult's directions. Nothing in this activity controls your device's power. Reset returns only the pretend session to its beginning so that you can rehearse the order again.

Make it real

Session sequence cards

You need: Paper and crayons

  1. Draw cards for adult readiness, power and waiting.
  2. Add cards for making, saving and closing a drawing.
  3. Add shutdown and waiting, then explain the full order.

Notice how saving protects work before the session ends.

Little questions, big discoveries

Does the power button here control my device?

No. Every action belongs to a pretend computer drawn in the lesson.

Are all real shutdown menus identical?

No. Devices differ. Follow the routine your teacher or supervising adult gives you.

Why save before closing?

Saving keeps the current work. Closing a window is a separate action and may otherwise leave unsaved changes.

Talk about it: parents & teachers

At home

  • Rehearse the sequence with paper cards and discuss which actions need adult supervision.
  • Let your child explain one change in the model before offering help. Give time for another attempt.

In the classroom

  • Ask learners to explain the reason for each action while a partner moves a marker through the sequence.
  • Ask pairs to predict, take turns with the controls, and describe the evidence they observed. Use the three challenges to identify a point to revisit.
