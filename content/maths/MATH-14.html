What’s going on here?

A metre is a common unit for measuring length. A rope marked at two points one metre apart can help compare objects around us. The screen shows the length between two knots. Its pale guide remembers the whole metre while you fold the rope. We are using a drawing of a measured rope, so the picture on your screen is not itself one metre long.

Fold the metre rope in half by bringing its ends together. The folded bundle reaches half a metre from end to end and has two equal layers. Fold it in half once more. The bundle now reaches a quarter metre and has four equal layers. The whole rope has not become shorter; its length is arranged in layers.

Choose a sample object and align the left ends before comparing. If its far end reaches beyond the reference, the object is longer than that reference. If it stops before the far end, it is shorter. If the ends match, the lengths are equal. An unaligned start can trick your eyes, so the alignment control is an important part of the check.

Try the same sample with the metre, half-metre and quarter-metre references. The seat-edge sample matches half a metre, but it is shorter than the full metre and longer than a quarter metre. These descriptions can all be true because the reference changed. Real seats and ribbons have many different lengths; our sample names do not claim that every such object has this size. At home or school, ask an adult to mark a rope using a proper metre measure. Compare light objects without moving heavy furniture. Explain both what you measured and which reference you used. That makes your comparison clear to another person.

Make it real

A reference ribbon

You need: A metre measure, soft ribbon and adult help

  1. Ask an adult to mark two points one metre apart on a ribbon.
  2. Fold the marked length in half and then in half again.
  3. Compare the folded references with a light book or tray.

Keep ribbon away from necks and do not move heavy furniture.

Little questions, big discoveries

Is the screen rope really one metre long?

No. It is a scaled drawing; use a real metre measure for real objects.

Can one object be longer and shorter?

It can be longer than one reference and shorter than a different reference.

Do all seat edges measure half a metre?

No. That is only the chosen sample in this activity.

Talk about it: parents & teachers

At home

  • Use a measured reference instead of guessing from arm length.
  • Ask the child to name the reference in each comparison.

In the classroom

  • Show the distinction between total rope and folded reach.
  • Avoid decimal labels and centimetre conversions here.
