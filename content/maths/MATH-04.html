What’s going on here?

On a number line, numbers grow as you move to the right. A card showing 432 belongs farther right than a card showing 342. The screen places each card above its correct point so that you can inspect it. Your job is to arrange the row of cards below from smallest to largest. Select a card, then move it left or right.

Hundreds, tens and ones help you explain the order. Compare 342 and 432. Four hundreds are more than three hundreds, so 432 is larger. There is no need to begin with the ones. Now compare 342 and 324. Both have three hundreds. Four tens are more than two tens, so 342 is larger than 324.

If the hundreds and tens match, compare the ones. For instance, 346 comes after 342 because six ones are more than two ones. If all three digits match, the amounts are equal. Equal cards may sit beside each other in either order. The place-value clues under the line help you check each part of a number rather than guessing from its last digit.

Change one card and decide whether your order needs changing. Replacing the hundreds digit can move a card a long way. A small change to the ones can matter when two cards are close. Zooming changes the portion of the line you see; it does not change any number or reverse their order. Read the end labels to see the range shown. Our full line runs from zero to 1000, with 1000 as its end milestone. Describe which place first decided your comparison. A clear reason is more helpful than saying that a number simply looks bigger.

Learning outcomeI can put numbers in order and explain which is larger.

Make it real

Card parade

You need: Three paper cards and a pencil

  1. Write 216, 261 and 206 on separate cards.
  2. Arrange them from smallest to largest.
  3. Change one digit and explain whether its place in the row changes.

Use invented numbers; no personal information is needed.

Little questions, big discoveries

Does the biggest last digit win?

No. Compare hundreds first, then tens, then ones if needed.

Can two cards share one point?

Yes. Equal values belong at the same number-line position.

Does a wider gap after zooming mean a new value?

No. Check the line labels: the view changed, not the numbers.

Talk about it: parents & teachers

At home

  • Ask which place first made the numbers different.
  • Let the child correct the order after changing one digit.

In the classroom

  • Include comparisons with matching hundreds and tens.
  • Use the milestone 1000 without adding four-digit operations.
