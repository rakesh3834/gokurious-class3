What’s going on here?

A number such as 234 can tell us about hundreds, tens and ones. Two hundred tiles represent 200. Three ten rods represent 30. Four small tiles represent 4. Together they show 200 plus 30 plus 4. The numeral is 234, and we say two hundred and thirty four. These are different ways to describe the same amount.

Begin with a number in the starting box. The trays show one usual arrangement of its blocks. Add a block by tapping its button or dragging it into the trays. A hundred tile adds one hundred, even though it is only one tile. A ten rod adds ten. Count the value each block represents, rather than counting every block as one.

An exchange is different from an addition. Ten one tiles can be traded for one ten rod. Both sides of that trade have the same value. Likewise, ten ten rods can become one hundred tile. Watch the numeral and number name as you exchange. They stay the same because no amount was added or taken away. Only its arrangement changed.

You can also unpack a ten to see ten ones again. For example, 234 can be shown with two hundreds, two tens and fourteen ones. That arrangement still totals 234. Regroup the ones to return to the usual form. A zero in a number can show that a place has no blocks: 304 has no tens in its usual arrangement. Explore small and large numbers up to 999. The next milestone is 1000, but our trays stop before it. Read your number aloud and explain an exchange to someone beside you.

Make it real

Paper place-value trays

You need: Paper, pencil and three labelled spaces

  1. Make large paper tiles labelled 100, strips labelled 10 and squares labelled 1.
  2. Build 126 in the three spaces.
  3. Exchange one ten strip for ten ones and explain why the amount stays equal.

The labels show pretend quantities; the paper sizes are just helpful reminders.

Little questions, big discoveries

Is one hundred tile just one?

It is one tile, but its labelled value is one hundred.

Can a tray have more than nine blocks?

During exchanges it can. Regrouping returns the usual hundreds, tens and ones arrangement.

Why are 304 and 340 different?

The 4 is in a different place: four ones in 304 and four tens in 340.

Talk about it: parents & teachers

At home

  • Ask the child to say both the numeral and its number name.
  • Use an exchange question before a written calculation.

In the classroom

  • Connect block value, expanded form and numeral each time.
  • Keep totals in 0–999; discuss 1000 only as the next milestone.
