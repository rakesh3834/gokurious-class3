Move the Clock

Move the clock in fifteen-minute steps. Watch the short hand move too, even when it has not reached the next hour.

Wonder about it: What if fifteen minutes pass after eight o’clock?

What’s going on here?

An analog clock shows time with hands moving around a numbered face. In this activity, the shorter purple hand shows hours and the longer amber hand shows minutes. At eight o’clock, the hour hand points to eight and the minute hand points to twelve. The written time is 8:00. Both displays describe the same moment.

Move forward fifteen minutes. The minute hand reaches three, and the written time becomes 8:15. We say quarter past eight. Notice the hour hand carefully: it has moved a little beyond eight. It does not wait at eight for the whole hour and then jump suddenly to nine. Both hands move as time passes, although the minute hand travels farther around the face.

Move another fifteen minutes to reach 8:30, or half past eight. The minute hand now points to six. The hour hand is halfway between eight and nine. The two moves together took thirty minutes. Moving back fifteen minutes returns to quarter past eight. The hand positions, written time and spoken label should always agree.

Choose another starting hour and repeat the same changes. At half past twelve, for example, the short hand lies between twelve and one, because the face returns to one after twelve. This activity focuses on o’clock, quarter past and half past, so the forward control stops at half past. Choose a new hour to begin another example. Look at a clock with an adult and identify which hand is longer before reading the time. The three on the face can help show fifteen minutes, not just the hour three. Explain the job of each hand instead of reading only the number it happens to point towards. That will help you understand why two hands are needed.

Learning outcomeI can show a time and see how both clock hands move.

Make it real

Two moving paper hands

You need: A drawn clock face and two paper strips of different lengths

  1. Lay the short strip towards eight and the long strip towards twelve.
  2. Show quarter past eight with both strips.
  3. Show half past eight and explain the short hand’s new position.

Move loose paper strips by hand; no sharp fastener is needed.

Little questions, big discoveries

Why does the hour hand move between numbers?

It moves gradually as minutes pass through the hour.

Does the three always mean three o’clock?

No. For the minute hand, that position marks fifteen minutes past the hour.

Why does the activity stop at half past?

These examples focus on o’clock, quarter past and half past.

Talk about it: parents & teachers

At home

  • Ask the child to inspect both hands at every step.
  • Use a clock with clearly different hand lengths.

In the classroom

  • Check the hour-hand position as well as the minute hand.
  • Avoid time zones and 24-hour notation in this lesson.
