What’s going on here?

Some male and female animals have different familiar names. A bull and a cow form one pair. A lion and a lioness form another. In this trail, each card tells you which name is the male name and which is the female name.

Choose a male animal name. Find its partner in the second list and join the pair. Hear the names together to help remember them. Our other pairs are drake and duck, cock and hen, and peacock and peahen. The small pictures identify an animal group; the written labels give the vocabulary we are learning.

Do not invent one ending to make every partner name. Lion and lioness share many letters, while bull and cow are different words. Learn each pair as a familiar vocabulary pair. Duck can also be used as a general bird name in other sentences; here it is the female name paired with drake. Try a wrong partner, read the explanation, then repair the connection and say the correct pair.

Learning outcomeI can recognise familiar paired animal names in spoken and written English.

Make it real

Animal pair trail

You need: Paper cards and a pencil.

  1. Write the ten names from the trail on separate cards.
  2. Match each male name with its taught female partner.
  3. Read two pairs to a partner and compare their spellings.

The vocabulary labels matter more than artistic detail in pictures.

Little questions, big discoveries

Does every female name end in -ess?

No. Cow, hen, duck and peahen show that this is not a general rule.

Does duck always mean only a female?

No. It can be a general bird name too. In this pair it is used beside drake for the female.

Can the picture alone tell me the correct word?

Use the labels and the heard pair; the pictures simply help identify animal groups.

Talk about it: parents & teachers

At home

  • Play a short call-and-response game with two or three pairs.
  • Invite the child to explain a choice, then replay or reread an example together.

In the classroom

  • Teach this limited vocabulary set without suffix generalisation.
  • Offer the tap or keyboard controls as well as dragging. Let partners take turns explaining before checking.
