What’s going on here?

Soil can hold a surprising mixture of things. You might notice tiny stones, old leaf pieces, roots and small animals. Some are easy to see and others can be hidden. Soil Window gives you two illustrated samples from a pretend place before and after rain. The pictures are examples for observation, not a promise about what every handful of soil contains.

Begin with the dry sample. Choose an object to inspect, then record it if it is visible. A small stone is not a living animal. A leaf piece came from a plant. A root belongs to a plant growing in the soil. An ant is an animal. Describing these differences helps us make a useful record instead of calling everything in the sample dirt.

Switch to the damp sample. It looks darker in this illustration, and an earthworm is visible. Rain can change what we notice, but it does not guarantee a certain number of animals. Some creatures may become easier to see; others may remain hidden. If you cannot find an earthworm in the dry picture, record that it is not visible there. Do not conclude that all dry soil is empty of life.

A fair comparison looks at the same place and uses the same careful method. Our two record rows help keep the observations separate. Real soil from another place may look different because its pieces and surroundings differ. Looking closely is useful, but safe habits matter too. An adult can choose a suitable observation spot. Leave animals and unknown objects alone, keep soil away from your mouth and wash hands afterwards. A drawing or photograph is also a good way to explore without handling soil.

Learning outcomeI can describe soil and recognise things found within it.

Make it real

Picture-based soil journal

You need: Paper, pencils and the two simulator views.

  1. Draw a dry-sample row and a damp-sample row.
  2. Sketch three visible objects in their matching rows.
  3. Circle an observation that differs between the pictures.

Say “I can see…” rather than guessing what every soil sample must contain.

Little questions, big discoveries

Is everything in soil alive?

No. Soil can contain rock pieces, remains of living things and living organisms.

Does rain create earthworms instantly?

No. Some animals may become easier to notice after rain.

Can I taste soil to compare it?

No. Keep it away from your mouth and use safe observation methods with an adult.

Talk about it: parents & teachers

At home

  • Distinguish “not visible” from “not present anywhere”.
  • Use the pictures if a safe outdoor observation is unavailable.

In the classroom

  • Ask children to separate observation from prediction.
  • Avoid soil horizons, pH and fixed animal-count claims.
