What’s going on here?

Turning on a tap is only one small part of a water journey. Water has come from somewhere and travelled in some way before reaching a home. Some families use pipes and tanks. Others carry water from a well or receive water from a tanker. Follow the Water offers three example stories so you can compare these different routes.

Choose a story and begin with its source. Next, select the way the water travels. Then connect the place where it is stored, followed by the home. The cards are deliberately mixed so you must think about their order. If you choose a later stop too soon, look again for the next part of the journey rather than starting over.

After all four stops are connected, follow the water from one stop to the next. In the well story, a person carries water in a container and it is stored in a covered pot. In the pipe story, water travels through pipes to a storage tank. The tanker story shows another way of bringing water to storage. These are examples; actual homes may have more steps or combine several methods.

A source is different from a storage place. A tank holds water, but we can still ask where its water came from. People work to collect, move and care for water so others can use it. The route alone cannot tell us whether water is safe to drink. That requires suitable care and adult guidance. At home, ask an adult to explain your water route from memory or a safe diagram. There is no need to climb to a roof, open a tank or go near a well to trace the story.

Little questions, big discoveries

Is a tank always the original source?

No. A tank usually stores water brought from somewhere else.

Does a clear-looking route prove water is safe?

No. Water safety needs suitable care; ask a responsible adult.

Can one home use more than one supply route?

Yes. A home may use different routes at different times.

Talk about it: parents & teachers

At home

  • Trace the household route through conversation.
  • Keep children away from wells, tanks and rooftops.

In the classroom

  • Welcome different local supply stories.
  • Assess sequence and source versus storage, not treatment engineering.
