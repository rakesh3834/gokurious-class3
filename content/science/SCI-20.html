What’s going on here?

A party can be fun without adding unnecessary throwaway items. Some choices can reduce what we use once, and some objects can be used again. Low-Waste Party Planner compares cups, shopping bags and decorations for one imaginary party. Changing a choice changes the example waste pile so you can see the connection.

The starting plan uses six single-use cups, two single-use bags and four throwaway decorations. Together these make twelve discarded items in the three selected categories. Choose reusable cups and the six cup items leave the pile. The cups are kept for washing and another use. They have not disappeared from the world; their next use is different.

Choose a cloth shopping bag and reuse stored decorations. The pile becomes smaller again. A cloth bag can be carried on another trip, and cared-for decorations can be brought out for another event. These are examples of reuse. Avoiding unnecessary new items also reduces the amount of waste created. The idea starts with the choice before something reaches a bin.

When all three choices are reusable, the counter reaches zero for these selected categories at this party. That does not mean the entire event has no waste or no use of resources. Food scraps, packaging from other things and cleaning needs may still exist. The numbers count illustrated items, not weight or environmental impact. Real reusable objects need safe care and should be suitable for their job. An adult can help choose clean, undamaged cups and appropriate cleaning. We can plan a thoughtful celebration by asking what is already available, what can be borrowed and what can be stored for next time, without buying new things just to complete an activity.

Make it real

Plan using what we have

You need: Paper, pencil and an adult to discuss available items.

  1. Draw a small celebration plan.
  2. Name one suitable item you can reuse or borrow.
  3. Say how it will be cleaned or stored for another use.

Plan on paper. Do not buy supplies or reuse damaged food containers for the activity.

Little questions, big discoveries

Does reuse mean using an item only once?

No. Reuse means using it again.

Does the counter measure weight or carbon emissions?

No. It counts illustrative throwaway items for one example party.

Is every old container suitable for food?

No. Ask an adult to choose clean, undamaged, suitable items.

Talk about it: parents & teachers

At home

  • Start with items already available and suitable.
  • Discuss cleaning and storage as part of reuse.

In the classroom

  • Compare one changed choice at a time.
  • Keep the conclusion about reduce and reuse, without footprint claims.
