What’s going on here?

We need water for many useful jobs, including keeping ourselves and our surroundings clean. Using water carefully means avoiding needless waste while still completing the job. Every Drop Helper compares two routines: brushing teeth and filling a bucket. The counters are made-up example units so you can focus on the effect of a choice.

Start with toothbrushing and run the routine with the tap left on. The example counter reaches ten units. Now choose the careful routine and run it again. The tap is off while the character brushes, so water is used only for the parts that need it. Our example uses two units instead of ten. The difference is eight example units saved.

Switch to bucket filling. When the tap keeps running after the bucket is full, extra water overflows. The careful routine stops at the useful fill level. In this example it uses six units rather than ten, saving four. The change is about when we stop the flow. It does not mean that a real bucket always holds six litres or that every household saves the same amount.

The numbers help compare the two illustrated routines, but they are not measurements from your home. Real use depends on the tap, container and task. You can describe the useful action without knowing an exact quantity: turn the tap off when flow is unnecessary, and avoid overflowing a container. Do not reduce needed handwashing or drinking to chase a small counter. An adult can help choose safe ways to avoid waste. Dirty water is not safe to drink, and water used for one job is not automatically suitable for another job or for watering plants.

Learning outcomeI can choose a daily action that wastes less water.

Little questions, big discoveries

Should I stop washing to save water?

No. Keep needed cleanliness habits and avoid unnecessary flow.

Are the counters in litres?

No. They are illustrative units used only in this activity.

Can I drink leftover cleaning water?

No. Drink water provided as safe by a responsible adult.

Talk about it: parents & teachers

At home

  • Discuss one avoidable flow at home without running a wasteful demonstration.
  • Treat the numbers as examples, not targets.

In the classroom

  • Compare identical tasks with one changed routine.
  • Keep saving water separate from drinking-water safety and health needs.
