What’s going on here?

Plants give us many familiar foods, and we do not eat the same part of every crop. A carrot is a root. Spinach provides leaves. A mango is a fruit, ginger is a stem and wheat provides seeds. Harvest the Plant lets you connect these five familiar foods with the parts from which they come.

Choose carrot first. The diagram shows a leafy top above the soil and an orange part below. Predict the part we eat, then harvest to reveal the label. Change to spinach and compare. Its edible leaves are above the soil in this picture. Both foods come from plants, but their plant-part labels are different. The word vegetable does not name just one plant part.

Look at mango and wheat next. A mango is the tree’s fruit. Wheat grains are seeds. In each case, follow the highlighted example rather than guessing that every edible part must be a root or a leaf. Ginger is a useful surprise. The part we use grows underground, but it is a stem. Being below soil does not automatically make a plant part a root.

These drawings are for identifying familiar food crops. They are not a guide for deciding whether an unknown leaf, berry or root is safe to eat. Many plants and plant parts are unsuitable for food. A responsible adult chooses and prepares food safely. You can practise the lesson by looking at familiar groceries, photographs or drawings without tasting anything. Explain your match in a full sentence, such as “Spinach gives us leaves.” If a crop has several useful parts, describe the particular food shown rather than claiming that every part of the plant has the same name or use.

Make it real

Plant-part food cards

You need: Paper, pencil and pictures of familiar foods.

  1. Draw carrot, spinach and mango on separate cards.
  2. Write Root, Leaf and Fruit on another set of cards.
  3. Match the cards and explain each pair.

Add ginger and wheat if you want another comparison. Do not sample unknown plants.

Little questions, big discoveries

Is every underground edible part a root?

No. Ginger is an example of an underground stem.

Does vegetable mean leaf?

No. Foods called vegetables can come from different plant parts.

May I taste a plant to identify it?

No. Identify with pictures or familiar foods chosen by an adult.

Talk about it: parents & teachers

At home

  • Ask for the plant-part name as well as the food name.
  • Use grocery pictures to avoid unsafe tasting.

In the classroom

  • Use the five unambiguous example matches.
  • Avoid detailed modified-organ anatomy.
