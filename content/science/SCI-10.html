What’s going on here?

A dish may look like one food, but it can contain several ingredients. To find its sources, we can trace those ingredients separately. Rice comes from a plant. Milk comes from an animal. Water and salt belong to neither of these two groups. Food Source Trail helps you follow each ingredient instead of guessing from the finished dish’s appearance.

Choose rice and dal, then open the dish. You will see plant ingredients together with water and salt. Tap an ingredient to follow its trail. The rice can be traced to the rice plant, and dal is made from pulses grown on plants. Water and salt still matter in a recipe, even though they do not fit the plant or animal labels.

Now choose rice pudding made with milk. Rice and sugar follow plant routes, while milk follows an animal route. A dish can therefore contain ingredients from both sources. The lassi example uses curd made from milk, sugar and water. Curd’s connection to milk helps us trace it back to an animal even though it looks different from plain milk.

These are example recipes rather than rules for every family. People prepare dishes in different ways and follow different diets. A dish with the same name may use a different ingredient in another home. Ask what actually went into it before classifying the sources. We can talk respectfully about food without deciding that everyone must eat the same things. This activity explains where ingredients come from; it does not ask you to taste an unfamiliar food. When discussing a real meal, use the ingredient information a trusted adult provides and keep allergies or family food needs in mind.

Make it real

Ingredient detective drawing

You need: Paper, pencil and information from an adult.

  1. Choose one familiar dish.
  2. Ask which ingredients were used and write or draw three.
  3. Trace each to Plant, Animal or Neither.

Different recipes can give different trails. Do not taste unknown ingredients.

Little questions, big discoveries

Can one dish use both plant and animal ingredients?

Yes. The milk rice pudding is one example.

Is curd a plant just because it is in a bowl?

No. In this example, curd is made from animal milk.

Do all families prepare the same dish in the same way?

No. Always check the actual ingredients.

Talk about it: parents & teachers

At home

  • Use one familiar recipe and invite respectful comparisons.
  • Ask about each ingredient separately.

In the classroom

  • Include the Neither group for water and salt.
  • Assess tracing rather than judging family diets.
