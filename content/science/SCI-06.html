What’s going on here?

A seed cannot walk to a new home, but it can move in other ways. An animal may carry a fruit or a nut away from a plant. When the seed is left behind, it has reached a different place. In this activity, a squirrel carries a nut. We follow the nut’s journey one moment at a time and think about what might happen next.

Start with the soil patch. The squirrel moves away from the parent tree and leaves the nut. Go to the next moment. If the squirrel does not return for it, the nut remains in that spot. A young plant may grow there when the seed is able to grow and its needs are met. Soil alone is not a guarantee that every seed will become a plant.

Change the destination to the hard paved path. In this story, no young plant grows there. The place does not provide a suitable growing space. Now try the option in which the squirrel returns and takes the nut. The seed is no longer left at that spot. Comparing these stories helps us see why carrying a seed and growing a plant are two different events.

The final picture shows one possible result, not a promise or an exact timetable. Real seeds may be eaten, damaged or left in unsuitable conditions. Growth takes time, even though our buttons move quickly between scenes. Animals help plants reach new places without planning to plant a garden. You can explain the idea by following three questions: Who carried the seed? Where was it left? Could it remain and grow there? Keep possible outcomes separate from things you have actually observed.

Learning outcomeI can describe one way an animal helps a plant spread.

Make it real

Draw a seed journey

You need: Paper and coloured pencils.

  1. Draw an animal carrying a seed.
  2. Draw a place where it leaves the seed.
  3. Draw two possible endings and explain why they could differ.

Use “may” for growth. No need to feed or approach wild animals.

Little questions, big discoveries

Do squirrels plant every nut on purpose?

No. Nuts left behind can sometimes grow without that being the animal’s intention.

Will every dropped seed grow?

No. The seed must be able to grow and have suitable conditions.

Do the buttons show exact days?

No. They show story moments, not a real growth clock.

Talk about it: parents & teachers

At home

  • Ask the child to narrate the four moments.
  • Discuss how changing one event changes the ending.

In the classroom

  • Assess simple seed movement and conditional growth.
  • Keep growth descriptions qualitative and avoid germination chemistry.
