What’s going on here?

A careful observer notices more than a leaf’s name. Leaves can differ in shape, size and colour. One may be long and small, while another is round and large. In Leaf Detective, each sample has a letter so you can follow it as the groups change. These illustrated samples let us practise describing visible features without needing to pick living leaves.

Start by sorting by shape. Long leaves join one group and round leaves join another. Look at Leaf A, then find Leaf B. The letters stay attached to the samples. Now change the rule to size. Large leaves move together, even when their colours or shapes differ. A leaf has several features at once, but a sorting rule asks us to focus on one of them.

Choose colour next. Green leaves gather together and brown leaves form another group. Changing the rule has not painted any leaf or made it grow. Only the way we organise the same collection has changed. If you are unsure, magnify one sample and read its features. The close-up makes the picture larger for your eyes; it does not change the sample’s size label.

Two leaves can match in colour and still differ in shape. They can match in size and still differ in colour. This is why we should explain our rule whenever we sort. If a friend makes different groups, ask which feature they used before deciding the answer is wrong. Real leaves have many possible shapes and shades beyond these simple examples. Outdoors, describe what you see and accept that some leaves may need a more careful description than one short label.

Make it real

Sort your leaf drawings

You need: Scrap paper and coloured pencils.

  1. Draw six imaginary leaves with different sizes, shapes and colours.
  2. Group the drawings by one visible feature.
  3. Keep the drawings unchanged and sort by a second feature.

Tell a partner the rule before asking them to add another leaf.

Little questions, big discoveries

Does magnifying make a small leaf a large leaf?

No. It makes the view bigger so you can inspect the same sample.

Must all leaves in one group look exactly alike?

No. They need to match the selected sorting feature.

Are all real leaves either long or round?

No. These are simple practice shapes; real leaves have many other outlines.

Talk about it: parents & teachers

At home

  • Ask for two different descriptions of the same sample.
  • Let the child choose a sorting rule for their own drawings.

In the classroom

  • Ask pairs to explain different valid groupings.
  • Keep comparisons at visible colour, shape and size.
