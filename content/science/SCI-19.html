What’s going on here?

Waste contains different kinds of things, so one route does not suit every item. Two-Bin Town practises separating clear examples using the green and blue bin colours in the textbook. Green is for the selected compostable food and leaf waste. Blue is for the selected clean recyclable materials. A separate adult-help route keeps special items out of both practice bins.

Choose vegetable peels or dry leaves. These are our green-bin examples. Suitable plant waste can break down over time in composting. It does not become finished compost the moment it enters a bin. Now choose the clean paper box or clean metal can. These are blue-bin examples that can be collected for recycling into other objects. Sorting keeps the different routes easier to manage.

Imagine mixing the clean paper box with wet vegetable peels. The paper may become dirty, making the next steps harder. Separating the clear examples at the start is more helpful than expecting a mixed pile to sort itself later. The activity gives a reason when a card is placed on the wrong route, so use the feedback to reconsider the material and its condition.

The battery card needs a different decision. Ask an adult to arrange the appropriate local collection route. It does not belong in either practice bin. Real collection systems can use other colours and more categories, so follow local guidance rather than assuming these two colours are universal. Children should practise with pictures, not handle unknown rubbish. Broken glass, needles, leaking batteries and other potentially harmful items need adult attention. The aim is to recognise a simple sorting idea and know when to ask for help, not to make every possible waste decision alone.

Learning outcomeI can separate common food/leaf waste from clean recyclable materials.

Make it real

Paper waste-sorting cards

You need: Scrap paper and pencils.

  1. Draw the five practice items without handling real waste.
  2. Make labels for Green, Blue and Ask an adult.
  3. Place each drawing by its route and explain why.

Ask an adult how your local collection system labels these routes.

Little questions, big discoveries

Are the bin colours the same everywhere?

No. Local collection systems may use different labels and categories.

Does compost appear immediately?

No. Composting takes time and suitable conditions.

Should I pick up a battery to sort it?

No. Ask an adult and practise using the picture card.

Talk about it: parents & teachers

At home

  • Explain your local collection labels.
  • Keep the activity picture-based for unknown or harmful waste.

In the classroom

  • Use clearly named compostable and clean recyclable examples.
  • Assess knowing when adult help is needed as well as bin matching.
