What’s going on here?

After rain, water does not stay in the same way on every surface. Some water soaks into soil. Some gathers in puddles or flows along the ground. Rain on My Street lets you compare two simple surfaces under the same illustrated shower. Before you start, make a prediction about the main change you expect to see.

Choose loose soil and start the shower. The blue marks below the surface show water soaking into the soil. A little water may still collect on top or move away. Now choose the paved path. More water remains above the surface or moves along it in this example. The hard covering allows much less water through than the loose soil shown beside it.

Keeping the shower the same helps us focus on the surface. If we changed the rain and the ground together, it would be harder to explain the difference. Your prediction can be useful even when it needs changing. Look at the result, describe the evidence and try again. A good explanation connects what you changed with what you observed.

Real places are more varied than our two pictures. Soil can be tightly packed, already wet or mixed with different materials. A slope can help water flow away, while a hollow can collect it. This is why the model uses words such as more and less instead of claiming exact amounts. It also does not say that all water always soaks into soil. When you safely watch rain from a sheltered place with an adult, look for soaking, collecting and flowing. These everyday observations are enough for this lesson; we do not need a full water-cycle explanation.

Learning outcomeI can compare water soaking into soil, collecting and flowing away.

Make it real

Rain-watching sketches

You need: Paper, pencil and a safe sheltered view with an adult.

  1. Look at one patch of ground after rain without going into traffic or flowing water.
  2. Draw where water has collected or flowed.
  3. Compare your observation with one simulator surface.

If rain is unavailable, use the simulator pictures and label your work as a model observation.

Little questions, big discoveries

Does all rain soak into every soil?

No. Soil condition, earlier rain and the slope can change what happens.

Are the blue lines real underground measurements?

No. They are picture symbols for water movement.

Should I test flowing water outdoors?

No. Watch from a safe place with an adult and stay away from drains, roads and floodwater.

Talk about it: parents & teachers

At home

  • Ask for a prediction before starting the shower.
  • Encourage “in this example” when explaining the result.

In the classroom

  • Keep the shower fixed while comparing surfaces.
  • Stay with soaking, puddles and flow; do not add water-cycle stages.
